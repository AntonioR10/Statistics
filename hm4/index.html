Theoretical Framework & Formal Derivations

1. Stochastic Penetration Increments

Let $X_1, X_2, \dots, X_n$ be independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables representing the penetration score assigned to security defenses at step $i$:

Bernoulli Model: $X_i \in \{0, 1\}$, with $P(X_i = 1) = p$.

Mean: $E[X_i] = p$, Variance: $Var(X_i) = p(1-p)$.

Rademacher Model: $X_i \in \{-1, +1\}$, with $P(X_i = +1) = 1/2$.

Mean: $E[X_i] = 0$, Variance: $Var(X_i) = 1$.

2. Law of Large Numbers (LLN)

The sample mean (average penetration score per system) is defined as $\bar{X}_n = \frac{S_n}{n} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n X_i$.

By linearity of expectation and independence:

$$E\left[\frac{S_n}{n}\right] = \mu, \quad Var\left(\frac{S_n}{n}\right) = \frac{\sigma^2}{n}$$

By Chebyshev's Inequality, for any $\varepsilon > 0$, $P\left(\left|\frac{S_n}{n} - \mu\right| \ge \varepsilon\right) \le \frac{\sigma^2}{n \varepsilon^2} \rightarrow 0$ as $n \rightarrow \infty$. Thus, $\frac{S_n}{n} \xrightarrow{P} \mu$.

3. Central Limit Theorem (CLT) & Scaling

While $\frac{S_n}{n}$ collapses to a single deterministic point $\mu$, scaling by $\sqrt{n}$ preserves the stochastic fluctuation width:

$$Z_n = \frac{S_n - n\mu}{\sigma \sqrt{n}} \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0, 1) \quad \text{as } n \rightarrow \infty$$

For the Rademacher model ($\mu = 0, \sigma = 1$), the normalized score $\frac{S_n}{\sqrt{n}}$ converges directly to standard normal $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$.

4. Cybersecurity & Risk Interpretation

  • System Scale ($n$): As infrastructure scales to $n$ systems, total penetration scores $S_n$ grow linearly $O(n)$, but maximum expected deviation bounds grow at rate $O(\sqrt{n})$.
  • Average Security Posture ($S_n/n$): According to LLN, an enterprise can reliably predict its mean vulnerability rate ($p$) across large systems despite random individual attacks.
  • Attacker Population ($m$): Simulating large $m$ trajectory runs maps out the full probability distribution envelope, enabling security teams to set upper percentile risk thresholds (e.g., $99^{\text{th}}$ percentile breach count).
